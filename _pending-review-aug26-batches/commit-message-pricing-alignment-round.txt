Align pricing copy with the real product; fix a real company-dedup bug

Two things, from Michael's direct follow-up on the customer-journey audit.

1) Pricing/marketing copy vs. code — resolved, not just flagged

Last round flagged three pricing-page claims with no matching code and
asked whether to build them or fix the copy. Found the answer already
sitting in the codebase itself, dated the same day as this work:
SupportWidget.jsx's system prompt was rewritten on 2026-08-26 specifically
because its old version "described features that don't exist" — and its
rewritten fact base already states plainly that there's no target-company
list, and that LinkedIn re-import works "for anyone, regardless of plan."
support-escalate.js's own header is equally direct: "There is no ticketing
system yet — per Michael's own call (2026-08-26), this goes straight to
his real inbox... since he's the entire support team today." All three
questions were already decided; the pricing page and entitlements.js just
hadn't been updated to match.

- src/lib/pricing.js: removed "LinkedIn re-import on demand" and "Priority
  support" from Growth's feature list (neither is real — re-import has no
  tier check anywhere in the code, and support has no priority queue when
  every ticket goes to the same one inbox). Removed "Shared target-company
  list" from Team's feature list — replaced with "Shared CRM across your
  team," which is real and already Team-exclusive by construction (a solo
  Starter/Growth account has no teammates to share with) but wasn't
  actually listed as a selling point anywhere. Reworded Growth's research
  bullet from "onboarding research pass" to "deeper, ongoing research
  scans" — entitlements.js's own comment confirms the deeper budget
  applies to the permanent background cron, not just a signup-day boost,
  which the old wording undersold.
- netlify/functions/lib/entitlements.js: removed the unused
  linkedinReimportOnDemand flag from TIER_LIMITS entirely, rather than
  leaving a dead gate with a misleading name sitting around for someone to
  wire up later on the strength of its name alone.
- src/components/SupportWidget.jsx: the system prompt had contradicted
  itself — its top-line plan summary still sold Growth on "LinkedIn
  re-import on demand" and "priority support" while its own more detailed
  sections, a few paragraphs down, already said neither is tiered. Fixed
  the summary to match the rest of the same prompt, and added an explicit
  line so Annie's support chat corrects a customer directly if asked about
  either as a paid perk, instead of contradicting itself depending which
  part of its own knowledge it draws from.

Login.jsx's "free trial, no credit card needed" line, flagged alongside

Marketing site pricing page (a separate repo, meetannie-deploy) wasn't

2) LinkedIn CSV import — real company-deduplication bug found and fixed

Michael asked me to specifically re-verify the "two contacts from the same
company, only one company record gets created" behavior. It doesn't
always hold. runImport() in src/pages/LinkedInImport.jsx built its list of
company names to create via `[...new Set(toImport.map(c => c.company))]`
— a Set on the RAW company string from the CSV, then checked each of
those raw names against existing companies with a single one-shot filter
over the whole list. Two contacts in the same import batch whose CSV rows
spelled the same company slightly differently ("Acme Trading" vs "acme
trading," or a stray trailing space — both realistic in real LinkedIn
export data) were treated as two different names by the Set, and the
filter checked both against the same not-yet-updated snapshot of what
already exists — so neither ever saw the other, both got queued, and the
batch insert created two separate company rows for one real company.
Confirmed against the live schema (no unique constraint on companies.name,
only a plain non-unique index) that nothing at the database level would
have caught this either.

Both contacts still end up correctly linked to a real company afterward
(companyMap's last-write-wins rebuild after the insert means they both
point at whichever of the two rows got created last) — so nothing looked
broken to a customer looking at their own contacts. But the other row sits
in Companies with zero contacts forever, which is exactly the "same
client never gets added twice" duplication Companies.jsx's own page copy
promises can't happen.

Fixed by deduplicating company names by their normalized (trimmed,
lowercased) form up front, before anything gets checked against existing
companies — every casing/whitespace variant of one company now collapses
to a single canonical entry (the first one seen in the CSV) before the
create-or-reuse decision is ever made, closing the gap for good rather
than just for the cases already caught by the existing-company lookup.

Full test suite (736 tests) and the production build both pass.
